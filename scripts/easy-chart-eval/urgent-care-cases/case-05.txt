Forty-five-year-old male here after cutting his left hand while slicing vegetables about an hour ago, the knife slipped. Bleeding is now controlled with direct pressure. No numbness in the fingers, able to move all fingers. Last tetanus shot was more than ten years ago. On exam there is a two and a half centimeter laceration across the left palm at the base of the index finger, edges are clean, no foreign body or debris, no tendon involvement with full flexion and extension against resistance, sensation intact to light touch in all fingers, capillary refill brisk. Wound was irrigated copiously, anesthetized with lidocaine, and closed with four interrupted nylon sutures. Tetanus booster was administered given it had been over ten years. Diagnosis hand laceration, repaired. Discussed keeping the wound clean and dry, daily dressing changes, and suture removal in ten to fourteen days. Return precautions discussed including spreading redness, warmth, swelling, pus, red streaking, fever, increasing pain, or numbness. Follow up with primary care for suture removal or sooner if any signs of infection.
