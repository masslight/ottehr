Fifty-two-year-old male here after twisting his right ankle stepping awkwardly off a curb about three hours ago, the ankle rolled inward. Heard no pop. Has been able to bear some weight but with significant pain and limping. Applied ice and took ibuprofen before arrival. On exam there is swelling and tenderness over the lateral malleolus and the anterior talofibular ligament, no tenderness over the posterior edge or tip of either malleolus and no tenderness at the base of the fifth metatarsal or navicular, neurovascularly intact, able to take a few steps. By the Ottawa ankle rules imaging was not strictly required, however given the swelling x-rays of the right ankle were obtained and are unremarkable with no fracture. Diagnosis lateral ankle sprain. Provided an air-stirrup ankle brace and advised rest, ice, compression, and elevation, with weight bearing as tolerated. Ibuprofen as needed for pain. Demonstrated ankle strengthening exercises to begin in about two weeks. Return precautions discussed including inability to bear weight, numbness, or worsening swelling. Follow up with primary care if not improving in five to seven days.
