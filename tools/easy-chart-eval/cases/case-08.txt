Four-month-old female here with three days of congestion and a wet cough, now breathing a little faster and feeding more slowly. Born full term, no underlying conditions. Older sibling has a cold. Still making wet diapers, no fever today. On exam there is copious nasal congestion, scattered wheezes and crackles bilaterally, mild subcostal retractions, sat 96 percent on room air, she is alert and consolable. Nasal suctioning was performed with improvement in work of breathing and she fed better afterward. RSV swab was positive. Diagnosis is bronchiolitis. Discussed that this is viral and antibiotics are not indicated, supportive care with nasal saline and suctioning especially before feeds, small frequent feeds, and monitoring hydration. Return precautions discussed including fast or labored breathing, retractions, pauses in breathing, blue color, fewer than normal wet diapers, or poor feeding — advised to seek emergency care for significant respiratory distress. Follow up with pediatrician in one to two days as bronchiolitis often peaks around days three to five.
