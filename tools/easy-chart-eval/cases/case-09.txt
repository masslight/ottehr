Sixty-year-old male with a known history of COPD here with three days of worsening shortness of breath and a cough productive of more sputum than usual, now yellow-green in color. Former smoker with a significant pack-year history. Using his albuterol inhaler more frequently with limited relief. No chest pain, no fevers, no leg swelling. On exam he is speaking in short phrases, there is a prolonged expiratory phase with diffuse wheezes and scattered rhonchi, mild accessory muscle use, oxygen saturation 92 percent on room air. Gave a combined albuterol and ipratropium nebulizer treatment with improvement in air movement and his saturation came up to 95. Diagnosis acute exacerbation of COPD. Started prednisone 40 mg daily times five days and azithromycin, and advised increased use of his albuterol. Reinforced smoking cessation counseling. Return precautions discussed including worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, blue lips, or fever, advised to seek emergency care for those. Follow up with primary care or pulmonology in two to three days.
