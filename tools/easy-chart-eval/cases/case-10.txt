Five-year-old male brought in after falling off his scooter onto pavement, has a laceration to the left forehead. Was wearing a helmet, no loss of consciousness, no vomiting, no behavior change, moving all extremities normally. Tetanus is up-to-date. On exam there is a two centimeter linear laceration above the left eyebrow, edges are clean and approximated, no foreign body or debris seen, no underlying step-off and no bony tenderness, neuro exam is normal. Wound was irrigated copiously and closed with skin adhesive given the clean linear nature and cooperative patient. Discussed keeping the area clean and dry for 24 hours, avoid picking or scrubbing, and that the glue will flake off on its own in five to ten days. Return precautions discussed including spreading redness, drainage, fever, wound reopening, or any signs of infection. Also reviewed head injury precautions including repeated vomiting, worsening headache, confusion, or difficulty waking. Follow up with pediatrician as needed.
