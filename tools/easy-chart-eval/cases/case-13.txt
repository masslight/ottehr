Twenty-four-year-old female here with an itchy raised rash that started about two hours ago, mom notes she took her first dose of a sulfa antibiotic this morning that was prescribed elsewhere for a sinus infection. The welts are on her trunk and arms, no swelling of the lips or tongue, no trouble breathing, no throat tightness, no wheezing, no vomiting or lightheadedness. No prior known drug allergies. On exam there are diffuse blanching urticarial wheels over the trunk and upper extremities, no facial or lip swelling, lungs are clear with no wheeze or stridor, and vital signs are stable. Diagnosis acute urticaria, an allergic reaction to the sulfa antibiotic. Gave diphenhydramine in the clinic with improvement, advised to stop the sulfa antibiotic immediately and use cetirizine daily for itching. Given the drug reaction, prescribed an epinephrine auto-injector and demonstrated its use, with instructions to use it and call 911 for any throat tightness, trouble breathing, lip or tongue swelling, or dizziness. Advised to add sulfa to her allergy list. Follow up with primary care and discussed an alternative non-sulfa antibiotic for the sinus infection.
