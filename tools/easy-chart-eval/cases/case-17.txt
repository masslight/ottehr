Fifty-five-year-old female here after slipping on ice and falling onto an outstretched right hand about two hours ago, complaining of wrist pain, swelling, and a deformity. No numbness in the fingers, able to wiggle them. On exam there is dorsal swelling and tenderness over the distal radius with a mild dinner-fork deformity, no break in the skin, sensation intact and capillary refill brisk in all fingers, range of motion limited by pain. X-rays of the right wrist were obtained and show a distal radius fracture, minimally displaced, with no obvious intra-articular extension. Diagnosis distal radius fracture, likely Colles type. Placed in a volar splint and sling for immobilization and comfort. Pain controlled with ibuprofen and acetaminophen. Advised to keep the arm elevated and to follow up with orthopedics within one week for definitive management and possible casting. Return precautions discussed including numbness, fingers turning pale or blue, severe or increasing pain, or the splint feeling too tight.
