Thirty-three-year-old female here with sudden onset of severe right flank pain that started about four hours ago and comes in waves, radiating down toward the right groin. Associated nausea and one episode of vomiting, and she noticed pink-tinged urine. No fever, no vaginal bleeding. She has a history of a kidney stone two years ago that passed on its own. On exam she is restless and unable to find a comfortable position, there is right costovertebral angle tenderness, abdomen is soft without rebound or guarding. Urine dip shows large blood, and a pregnancy test is negative. Diagnosis renal colic, consistent with a kidney stone. Pain was managed with intravenous ketorolac and an antiemetic with significant relief, encouraged oral fluids, and advised to strain the urine to catch the stone. Started tamsulosin to help with passage. Discussed that most small stones pass on their own over days. Return precautions discussed including fever or chills which could indicate an infected obstructing stone, intractable pain or vomiting, or inability to urinate, advised to go to the emergency department for those. Follow up with primary care or urology, and discussed imaging if symptoms persist.
